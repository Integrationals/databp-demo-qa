The business

Market data licensing from the exchange to the subscriber, and where reconciliation fits.

DataBP POC /

Exchanges sell the market data their trading produces, through vendors, to subscribers who use it to trade and to protect their money. Four documents describe each subscriber's use; DataBP builds software for the sellers, and our app checks that the four agree.

#Who or whatIn short
1The exchangeProduces market data and licenses it to vendors
2The vendorDistributes the data, permits subscribers, reports their use
3The subscriberPays to trade, value holdings, manage risk, prove best execution
4The four documentsLicense, entitlement, usage report, invoice
5DataBP and our appSoftware for the sellers; the app checks the four agree
  1. An exchange is a market where shares, bonds, options and futures trade, and it sells the data its trading produces. In its market data agreement it grants a license to a data recipient: limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable and revocable, for the agreement's term, to use the data and to distribute it to subscribers who sign a subscriber agreement.
  2. Vendors include market data vendors, broker-dealers and private network providers. A vendor permits each subscriber to receive the data through an entitlement control the exchange approves, and each month submits a usage report to the exchange counting the units each subscriber used, sometimes from counts the subscriber supplies.
  3. Subscribers pay because the data feeds decisions that make or protect money: people trading from screens (display use, charged per screen); machines trading by algorithm, order routing or market making (non-display use, charged apart); pricing and valuing what they hold; watching risk during the day; and, for brokers, best execution, the duty to obtain the best terms for customers' orders, measured against the NBBO, the national best bid and national best offer: for a stock, the highest priced bid and the lowest priced offer across the exchanges quoting it, and the one price the business's operational side is checked against.
  4. Each subscriber's use is described by four documents: the license, what was granted; the entitlement, what is permitted; the usage report, what was used, in units of count; and the invoice, what was billed, by the exchange or by the vendor passing the fees on. They should agree.
  5. DataBP builds software for the sellers: exchanges, trading platforms, index and data providers, data vendors and fintechs. It turns sales opportunities in Salesforce into licensing agreements and offers its Licensing Ledger, which since 2026-10-08 the London Stock Exchange's customers use to check their entitlements against their invoices: entitlement and billing reconciliation. Our app, under the same name, does it for one seller: it compares the four documents for each of its subscribers, shows each disagreement with its dollar amount, and what changed since the last month-end close.
